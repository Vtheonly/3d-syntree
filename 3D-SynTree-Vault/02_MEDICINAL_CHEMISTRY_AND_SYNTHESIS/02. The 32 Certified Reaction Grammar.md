# 02. The 32 Certified Reaction Grammar

### 1. What It Is

The certified reaction grammar is a standardized set of high-yield, medically validated chemical reactions that 3D-SynTree permits during molecular assembly. Rather than allowing any theoretically possible organic transformation, the model is restricted to reaction templates known to work reliably in the laboratory—typically with yields above 90% under standard conditions.

The grammar consists of reaction SMARTS templates, each defining:
- **Reactant patterns:** What functional groups must be present on each partner
- **Product pattern:** What bond is formed and what atoms are preserved
- **Leaving groups:** What fragments are ejected during the reaction
- **Compatibility rules:** Which handle pairs can react with which

3D-SynTree implements 10 certified reaction classes (the original "32" refers to the broader Hartenfeller et al. collection from which these are drawn). Each reaction family corresponds to a specific medicinal chemistry transformation commonly used in drug discovery.

### 2. Why It Exists

Most theoretical organic reactions fail in practice. They require exotic catalysts, extreme temperatures, inert atmospheres, or produce complex mixtures. A generative model that proposes such reactions designs molecules that cannot actually be made—the "synthesizability wall."

By restricting the action space to certified reactions, 3D-SynTree ensures:

- **Forward synthetic validity:** Every proposed bond can be formed in the lab
- **High yield:** Reactions are selected for reliability, not just theoretical possibility
- **Medicinal chemistry relevance:** The reactions are those actually used to make approved drugs
- **Computational tractability:** A finite set of templates is easier to enumerate than infinite chemical space

### 3. How It Works

Each certified reaction follows the SMARTS format:

`[Reactant 1 pattern].[Reactant 2 pattern]>>[Product pattern]`

Atom-mapped atoms (denoted with `:n`) are tracked through the reaction, preserving 3D coordinate provenance. Unmapped atoms are recreated by RDKit and lose their coordinate history.

The 10 certified reaction families:

| # | Reaction Family | Transformation | Example Use |
|---|----------------|----------------|-------------|
| 1 | Amide coupling | Carboxylic acid + Amine → Amide | Most common drug linkage |
| 2 | Sulfonamide coupling | Sulfonyl chloride + Amine → Sulfonamide | Celecoxib, sildenafil, darunavir motif |
| 3 | Reductive amination | Aldehyde/Ketone + Amine → Alkylated amine | C–N bond formation |
| 4 | Aryl amination (Buchwald-Hartwig / SNAr) | Aryl halide + Amine → Aryl amine | Aniline derivatives |
| 5 | Suzuki-Miyaura | Aryl halide + Boronic acid → Biaryl | C–C bond between aromatics |
| 6 | sp³ Alkylation | Alkyl halide + Nucleophile → Substituted alkane | Saturated C–N or C–O |
| 7 | Urea formation | Amine + Amine + Carbonyl → Urea | Kinase inhibitor hinge binders |
| 8 | Esterification | Carboxylic acid + Alcohol → Ester | Prodrugs, polar groups |
| 9 | Click triazole | Alkyne + Azide → 1,2,3-Triazole | Bioorthogonal, rigid linker |
| 10 | (Extended family) | Additional variants | Project-specific expansions |

**Key reactions added in Priority 4:**
- **Sulfonamide coupling:** R–SO₂Cl + R'–NH₂ → R–SO₂–NH–R'. The most common motif in FDA-approved drugs (celecoxib, sildenafil, darunavir).
- **sp³ Alkylation:** R–CH₂–X + R'–NH₂ → R–CH₂–NH–R'. Saturated C–N bond formation, improves Fsp³.

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Reaction template | SMARTS string defining reactants → product |
| Reaction family | Named class (e.g., "amide_coupling") |
| Handle pairing | Compatible reactive handles on each reactant |
| Leaving group | Fragment ejected (–OH, –Cl, –H, etc.) |
| Atom mapping | `:n` labels tracking atoms through reaction |
| REACTION_TEMPLATES | Dictionary of all templates in code |
| REACTION_FAMILY_MEMBERS | Grouping of related reactions |
| Reaction head | Neural network predicting reaction family |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **If no template fits, the connection is masked.** When the policy considers attaching a synthon to a handle, it checks whether any certified reaction can join those two handle types. If none can, that action is assigned probability zero. This is a hard constraint, not a soft preference.

- **Atom mapping preserves 3D provenance.** Mapped atoms (`:1`, `:2`, `:3`) keep their isotope labels through RDKit's reaction execution, allowing the conformer engine to reconstruct 3D coordinates for the product. Unmapped atoms are recreated fresh and lose coordinate history.

- **The grammar is append-only.** New reactions (sulfonamide coupling, sp³ alkylation) were added at the end of the template list to preserve index compatibility with existing datasets. You never insert or reorder existing entries.

- **Common pitfall:** Assuming that because two functional groups exist in a molecule, they will react. The reaction grammar defines specific handle pairs—an alcohol and an aldehyde do not automatically form anything under this grammar.

- **Common pitfall:** Confusing "certified" with "universal." These reactions work well for typical drug-like substrates but may fail with highly sterically hindered or electronically unusual cases.

### 7. Project Connection

The certified reaction grammar maps directly to the 3D-SynTree codebase:

- **`REACTION_TEMPLATES`** in `syntree/chemistry/reactions.py`: Dictionary of 10 SMARTS templates, each with atom-mapped reactants and products.

- **`REACTION_FAMILY_MEMBERS`** in `syntree/chemistry/reactions.py`: Groups related reactions into families for the reaction head.

- **`REACTION_SIDES`** in `syntree/chemistry/reactions.py`: Maps each reaction family to its two compatible handle sides (e.g., amide_coupling → carboxylic_acid + primary_secondary_amine).

- **Reaction head** (`syntree/models/reaction_head.py`): Predicts reaction family probabilities given the current handle and pocket context. The output feeds into synthon selection.

- **`apply_reaction()`, `_tag_atoms()`, `_build_result()`** in `syntree/chemistry/reactions.py`: Execute RDKit reactions with isotope tagging for coordinate provenance.

**Essential.** The reaction grammar defines the boundary of what 3D-SynTree can chemically produce.
