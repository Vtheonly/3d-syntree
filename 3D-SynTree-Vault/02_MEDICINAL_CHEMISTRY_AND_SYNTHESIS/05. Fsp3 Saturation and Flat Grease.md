# 05. Fsp3 Saturation and Flat Grease

### 1. What It Is

The **Fsp³ saturation metric** quantifies the fraction of carbon atoms in a molecule that are sp³-hybridized (tetrahedral, saturated) relative to the total number of carbon atoms:

$$Fsp^3 = \frac{\text{Number of } sp^3 \text{ Carbons}}{\text{Total Carbon Count}}$$

A value of 0.0 means all carbons are sp²-hybridized (aromatic, alkene, carbonyl—flat). A value of 1.0 means all carbons are sp³-hybridized (alkane—fully saturated, 3D).

The term **"flat grease"** describes the tendency of traditional medicinal chemistry to produce molecules dominated by flat, aromatic, lipophilic structures. These compounds often show excellent in silico docking scores because aromatic rings stack favorably into flat hydrophobic pockets, but they fail in animal models due to poor aqueous solubility, high metabolic clearance, and non-specific off-target binding.

### 2. Why It Exists

In 2009, Lovering et al. analyzed ~3,500 drugs and clinical candidates and found a striking correlation: molecules with higher Fsp³ values had higher clinical success rates. This became known as the **"escape from flatland"** hypothesis.

The physical rationale:

- **sp³ carbons are tetrahedral**, introducing 3D volume, chiral vectors, and non-planar shapes
- **Flat aromatic molecules stack into crystal lattices**, reducing aqueous solubility
- **Flat molecules expose large hydrophobic surface areas**, promoting non-specific binding to off-target proteins
- **sp³-rich molecules disrupt crystal packing**, improving solubility and selectivity
- **Saturated frameworks resist metabolic oxidation**, improving half-life

3D-SynTree imposes an **Fsp³ ≥ 0.40 constraint** to force the model away from flat, greasy molecules and toward 3D-saturated, drug-like structures.

### 3. How It Works

#### Calculating Fsp³

For each carbon atom in a molecule, determine its hybridization:
- sp³: Carbon with four single bonds (e.g., alkane carbon, tetrahedral stereocenter)
- sp²: Carbon with one double bond or part of an aromatic ring (e.g., alkene, carbonyl, aromatic)
- sp: Carbon with a triple bond (e.g., alkyne, nitrile)

$$Fsp^3 = \frac{N_{sp^3}}{N_{total\_carbon}}$$

#### Examples

| Molecule | sp³ Carbons | Total Carbons | Fsp³ | Character |
|----------|-------------|---------------|------|-----------|
| Benzene | 0 | 6 | 0.00 | Fully flat aromatic |
| Cyclohexane | 6 | 6 | 1.00 | Fully saturated |
| Aspirin | 1 | 9 | 0.11 | Mostly aromatic |
| Ibuprofen | 3 | 13 | 0.23 | Partially saturated |
| Typical 3D-SynTree output | ≥ 0.40 × total | — | ≥ 0.40 | Drug-like saturation |

#### 3D-SynTree Implementation

The catalog filtering parameter `min_fsp3 = 0.42` in `syntree/chemistry/catalog.py` ensures that every synthon in the 50,000-entry library meets or exceeds the 0.40 thesis threshold (with a small safety margin).

The reward component in `syntree/engine/rl.py`:
$$R_{Fsp^3} = fsp3\_weight \times \tanh(\max(0, fsp3 - 0.42))$$

This provides a graded reward: molecules below 0.42 get zero Fsp³ reward; molecules above 0.42 get increasing reward up to a saturation point.

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Fsp³ metric | Fraction of sp³-hybridized carbons |
| Escape from flatland | Lovering et al. (2009) observation linking Fsp³ to clinical success |
| Flat grease | Pejorative term for flat, aromatic, lipophilic compounds |
| min_fsp3 = 0.42 | Catalog filtering threshold (slightly above 0.40 thesis) |
| tanh reward | Smooth, bounded reward function for Fsp³ saturation |
| sp³ carbon | Tetrahedral carbon with four single bonds |
| sp² carbon | Trigonal planar carbon with double/aromatic bond |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **Fsp³ is not about avoiding all aromatics.** A drug molecule can have aromatic rings (many do—most kinase inhibitors have at least one). The constraint is about balance: at least 40% of carbons should be sp³ to ensure 3D character.

- **The 0.40 threshold is empirical, not theoretical.** It comes from clinical success statistics, not from first-principles physics. Molecules with Fsp³ = 0.39 are not automatically bad, and molecules with Fsp³ = 0.41 are not automatically good.

- **sp³ carbons introduce chirality.** Each sp³ carbon with four different substituents is a potential stereocenter. 3D-SynTree must preserve stereochemistry during conformer generation (see [[03_SPATIAL_CHEMISTRY_AND_FORCE_FIELDS/01_Stereochemistry Chirality and CIP Rules]]).

- **Common pitfall:** Maximizing Fsp³ at the expense of all else. A molecule with Fsp³ = 1.0 but no polar groups cannot cross membranes or form hydrogen bonds. Fsp³ is one component of drug-likeness, not the whole story.

- **Common pitfall:** Confusing Fsp³ with molecular weight or ring count. A large sp³-rich molecule can still be flat if it consists of fused cyclohexane rings in a planar arrangement.

### 7. Project Connection

The Fsp³ concepts map directly to the 3D-SynTree codebase:

- **`FractionCSP3(mol)`** in `syntree/chemistry/catalog.py` and `syntree/data/curation.py`: Computes Fsp³ using RDKit's `rdMolDescriptors.CalcFractionCSP3()`.

- **`min_fsp3 = 0.42`** in `syntree/chemistry/catalog.py`: Catalog filtering parameter. Only synthons with Fsp³ ≥ 0.42 enter the 50,000-entry library.

- **`fsp3_weight * tanh(max(0, fsp3 - 0.42))`** in `syntree/engine/rl.py`: Reward component in `ThreeDReward` that incentivizes Fsp³ saturation.

- **`min_fsp3 = 0.40`** in `scripts/build_synthon_catalog.py`: The biological rationale documented in the catalog build script.
