# 04. Lennard Jones and Soft-Core Potentials

### 1. What It Is

The **Lennard-Jones 6-12 potential** is a mathematical model of the interaction energy between two non-bonded atoms as a function of their separation distance:

$$V_{\text{LJ}}(r) = 4\epsilon \left[ \left( \frac{\sigma}{r} \right)^{12} - \left( \frac{\sigma}{r} \right)^6 \right]$$

This potential captures two competing physical effects:

- **Pauli repulsion** (r⁻¹² term): At very short range, electron clouds overlap and repulsion rises extremely steeply
- **London dispersion attraction** (r⁻⁶ term): At medium range, fluctuating dipoles create weak attraction

The **soft-core approximation** modifies the Lennard-Jones potential to avoid the mathematical singularity at r → 0, replacing the infinite repulsive wall with a bounded, continuous function suitable for machine learning gradients.

### 2. Why It Exists

Atoms interact through a balance of repulsion and attraction. The Lennard-Jones potential is the standard classical model for this balance. However, the raw potential has a critical flaw for machine learning:

**The singularity problem:** As r → 0, the r⁻¹² term diverges to +∞. In gradient-based optimization, this creates exploding gradients that destabilize training. A single atom pair that drifts too close during conformational search can produce infinite loss and NaN gradients.

The soft-core solution replaces the infinite wall below a switch distance (typically r < 0.8σ) with a linear or bounded continuation. This keeps gradients finite while preserving the physical behavior at relevant distances.

### 3. How It Works

#### Lennard-Jones Parameters

| Parameter | Symbol | Physical Meaning |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| Well depth | ε | Energy minimum depth (attraction strength) |
| Collision diameter | σ | Distance where potential crosses zero |
| Equilibrium distance | r_min = 2^(1/6)σ | Distance at minimum energy (−ε) |

At r = r_min = 2^(1/6)σ ≈ 1.12σ, the potential reaches its minimum of −ε. This is the preferred non-bonded contact distance.

#### The Singularity Problem

As r → 0:
- (σ/r)¹² → ∞
- V_LJ → +∞
- dV/dr → −∞ (infinite repulsive gradient)

In machine learning, this means:
- Loss can become infinite
- Gradients can overflow to NaN
- Optimizer steps become unstable

#### Soft-Core Solution

Below a switch distance r_switch = 0.8σ, the r⁻¹² repulsive wall is replaced with a linear or bounded function. 3D-SynTree implements a C¹-continuous linearized Lennard-Jones potential:

- For r ≥ 0.8σ: Full Lennard-Jones potential
- For r < 0.8σ: Linearized repulsive wall with bounded slope

This ensures:
- Continuous energy (no jumps at the switch point)
- Continuous first derivative (C¹ continuity)
- Bounded gradients (no explosions)
- Physical behavior preserved at contact distances

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| ε (epsilon) | Well depth, attraction strength |
| σ (sigma) | Collision diameter, zero-crossing distance |
| r⁻¹² term | Pauli repulsion, dominant at short range |
| r⁻⁶ term | London dispersion attraction, dominant at medium range |
| r_min | Equilibrium distance (2^(1/6)σ) |
| Singularity | Infinite energy at r → 0 |
| Soft-core | Bounded replacement for infinite wall |
| Switch distance | Boundary between full and softened potential (0.8σ) |
| C¹ continuity | Continuous energy and first derivative |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **The Lennard-Jones potential has both attraction and repulsion.** Unlike a pure hard-sphere model, LJ rewards atoms at optimal contact distance (−ε) and penalizes both overlap (repulsion) and excessive distance (no attraction).

- **Soft-core is a numerical fix, not a physical approximation.** The softened region (r < 0.8σ) is physically unrealistic, but atoms should rarely be there anyway. The soft-core simply prevents numerical disasters when they do occur.

- **3D-SynTree uses soft-core for reward shaping.** The contact energy reward in `syntree/engine/rl.py` uses the soft-core LJ to reward good shape complementarity while penalizing clashes with bounded gradients.

- **Common pitfall:** Using raw LJ in gradient-based optimization without soft-core. The first clash creates NaN gradients and training collapses.

- **Common pitfall:** Confusing van der Waals radii (Bondi) with LJ σ. They are related but different parameters. Bondi radii are "contact" radii; LJ σ is the zero-crossing distance.
