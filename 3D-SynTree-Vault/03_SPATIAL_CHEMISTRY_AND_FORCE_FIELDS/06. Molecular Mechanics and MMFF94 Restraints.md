# 06. Molecular Mechanics and MMFF94 Restraints

### 1. What It Is

**Molecular mechanics (MM)** is a classical physics approach to modeling molecular systems. Instead of solving quantum mechanical equations (which are computationally expensive), MM treats atoms as balls and bonds as springs, using empirical potential energy functions to calculate a molecule's total energy.

The **Merck Molecular Force Field 1994 (MMFF94)** is a specific force field parameterized for drug-like organic molecules. It calculates potential energy as a sum of bonded terms (bond stretching, angle bending, torsion, out-of-plane bending) and non-bonded terms (van der Waals, electrostatics):

$$E_{\text{total}} = E_{\text{str}} + E_{\text{ang}} + E_{\text{tor}} + E_{\text{oop}} + E_{\text{vdW}} + E_{\text{elec}}$$

**Flat-bottom harmonic position restraints** are additional terms that keep selected atoms near reference positions:

$$E_{\text{restraint}} = \frac{1}{2} k \cdot \max(0, \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0\| - r_{\text{tol}})^2$$

This creates a "flat" region around the reference position where no restraint is applied, and a harmonic spring outside that region.

### 2. Why It Exists

After 3D-SynTree attaches a new synthon to a growing ligand, the junction bond may have minor strain, stretched bonds, or non-ideal angles. The ETKDG embedding produces a reasonable starting geometry, but it needs local relaxation to reach physical bond lengths and angles.

However, unconstrained force-field minimization is dangerous: it can pull the entire ligand out of its protein binding pose. The scaffold (already-bound portion) must stay fixed while only the new junction relaxes.

MMFF94 with flat-bottom harmonic restraints solves this by:
1. Holding core atoms tethered to their pocket coordinates
2. Allowing junction atoms to relax freely
3. Using a drug-like force field (MMFF94 is parameterized for exactly the kinds of molecules 3D-SynTree generates)

### 3. How It Works

#### Energy Terms

| Term | Symbol | Physical Meaning |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| Bond stretching | E_str | Deviation from equilibrium bond length |
| Angle bending | E_ang | Deviation from equilibrium bond angle |
| Torsion | E_tor | Rotation around single bonds (dihedral energy) |
| Out-of-plane | E_oop | Deviation from planarity at sp² centers |
| van der Waals | E_vdW | Non-bonded atomic contacts (Lennard-Jones) |
| Electrostatic | E_elec | Coulomb interactions between partial charges |

#### Flat-Bottom Restraint

The restraint creates a tolerance sphere around each restrained atom's reference position:

- **Inside radius r_tol:** Zero energy, atom can move freely
- **Outside radius r_tol:** Harmonic spring pulls atom back: E = ½k(d − r_tol)²

In 3D-SynTree, core atoms are restrained with k = 50 kcal/mol/Å², which is strong enough to prevent drift but weak enough to allow minor relaxation.

#### Minimization Algorithm

The energy is minimized using gradient-based methods (typically conjugate gradient or L-BFGS):
1. Calculate energy and gradients at current coordinates
2. Move atoms in the direction of steepest energy descent
3. Repeat until convergence (gradients below threshold)

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Force field | Empirical potential energy function |
| MMFF94 | Merck Molecular Force Field, parameterized for drug-like molecules |
| Bond stretching | Harmonic penalty for bond length deviations |
| Angle bending | Harmonic penalty for bond angle deviations |
| Torsion term | Periodic penalty for dihedral angle deviations |
| Out-of-plane bending | Penalty for non-planar sp² centers |
| Flat-bottom restraint | Tolerance-based position constraint |
| Force constant k | Spring stiffness (kcal/mol/Å²) |
| Conjugate gradient | Energy minimization algorithm |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **MMFF94 is classical, not quantum.** It does not model electron distribution or bond breaking/forming. It assumes fixed connectivity and calculates energy based on geometric deviations from ideal values.

- **Restraints are essential for scaffold anchoring.** Without restraints, MMFF94 minimization would relax the entire ligand, potentially pulling it out of the protein pocket. The flat-bottom design allows minor local adjustment while preventing global drift.

- **The force constant matters.** k = 50 kcal/mol/Å² is a typical value—strong enough to hold the scaffold, weak enough to allow junction relaxation. Too strong = no relaxation; too weak = scaffold drifts.

- **Common pitfall:** Running unconstrained minimization on a ligand in a pocket. This almost always degrades binding pose quality.

- **Common pitfall:** Using MMFF94 for charged systems without proper partial charges. MMFF94's electrostatic term depends on accurate charge assignment.

### 7. Project Connection

The molecular mechanics and MMFF94 concepts map directly to the 3D-SynTree codebase:

- **`_relax_with_harmonic_restraints()`** in `syntree/chemistry/conformer.py`: Applies 50 kcal/mol/Å² flat-bottom harmonic restraints to core atoms while leaving junction atoms free for relaxation.

- **MMFF94 force field** in `syntree/chemistry/conformer.py`: Used via RDKit's `MMFFGetMoleculeForceField` and `MMFFHasAllMoleculeParams` for energy minimization.

- **PoseBusters validation** (`syntree/chemistry/validator.py`): Checks that post-relaxation geometries remain physically valid (bond lengths within 0.1 Å, angles within 15°, etc.).

### 8. Required Depth

**Essential.** Restrained MMFF94 relaxation is the step that converts a synthetically valid but geometrically imperfect junction into a physically realistic 3D structure.
