# 02. Rotatable Bonds and Dihedral Angles

### 1. What It Is

A **dihedral angle** (or torsion angle, symbol φ) is the angle between two intersecting planes defined by four sequentially bonded atoms: A₁–A₂–A₃–A₄. It describes the rotation around the central A₂–A₃ bond.

A **rotatable bond** is a single bond (σ bond) around which rotation is physically possible without breaking covalent bonds. Not all single bonds are freely rotatable—steric hindrance, partial double-bond character (resonance), and ring constraints can restrict rotation.

Dihedral angles are defined on a continuous circle [−π, π) or [0°, 360°)—mathematically equivalent to the Lie group SO(2), the group of rotations in a plane.

### 2. Why It Exists

Single bonds allow molecular flexibility. Without rotatable bonds, molecules would be rigid objects unable to adopt the shapes needed to fit into protein binding pockets.

However, rotation is not free. As atoms rotate around a bond, they pass through energy barriers:
- **Eclipsed conformations:** Atoms align, creating steric and electronic repulsion (energy maximum)
- **Staggered conformations:** Atoms are maximally separated (energy minimum)
- **Gauche and anti:** Specific staggered arrangements with different steric profiles

3D-SynTree exploits the fact that instead of diffusing all 3N Cartesian coordinates of a new fragment, it can lock the fragment's internal geometry and predict only the dihedral angle around the newly formed bond. This reduces a complex 3D search to a 1D circular problem.

### 3. How It Works

#### Dihedral Definition

For atoms A₁–A₂–A₃–A₄:
1. Plane 1 is defined by A₁, A₂, A₃
2. Plane 2 is defined by A₂, A₃, A₄
3. The dihedral angle is the angle between these two planes, viewed along the A₂–A₃ axis

By convention:
- 0°: A₁ and A₄ are eclipsed (same side, cis-like)
- 180°: A₁ and A₄ are anti (opposite sides, trans-like)
- 60°: Gauche conformation
- −60°/300°: Other gauche conformation

#### SO(2) Circular Topology

The dihedral angle lives on a circle: −180° and +180° are the same physical angle. This creates statistical challenges:
- The Euclidean mean of 1° and 359° is 180° (wrong! the circular mean is 0°)
- Standard MSE loss penalizes crossing the −π/π boundary incorrectly
- Circular statistics (von Mises distribution) are required (see [[08_CIRCULAR_STATISTICS_AND_TORSION_HEADS/01_Statistics on Riemannian Manifolds SO2]])

#### Energy Profile

Rotating around a bond produces a periodic energy curve with:
- **Minima:** Staggered conformations (anti is usually lowest energy)
- **Maxima:** Eclipsed conformations (highest steric repulsion)
- **Barrier height:** Typically 3–15 kcal/mol for C–C single bonds, higher for bonds adjacent to bulky groups

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Dihedral angle (φ) | Angle between planes A₁–A₂–A₃ and A₂–A₃–A₄ |
| Rotatable bond | Single bond permitting rotation |
| SO(2) | Circle group, mathematical description of dihedral topology |
| Eclipsed | High-energy conformation with atoms aligned |
| Staggered | Low-energy conformation with atoms maximally separated |
| Gauche | Staggered conformation with 60° dihedral |
| Anti | Staggered conformation with 180° dihedral |
| Torsional strain | Energy penalty from eclipsing interactions |
| Rotamer basin | Energy well corresponding to a preferred conformation |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **3D-SynTree predicts only the junction dihedral.** When a new synthon attaches to a core, the synthon's internal geometry is fixed (from ETKDG embedding). The only degree of freedom is the rotation around the newly formed single bond. The policy predicts this one angle.

- **Not all single bonds are rotatable.** Amide bonds (partial double bond character), bonds in rings, and bonds adjacent to bulky groups may be effectively locked. 3D-SynTree's `is_conjugated_junction()` identifies these cases.

- **Dihedral angles wrap around.** A change from 179° to −179° is a 2° rotation, not a 358° rotation. Loss functions must respect circular continuity.

- **A 10° error matters.** At 7 Å from the junction, a 10° angular error sweeps an arc of 1.22 Å—enough to cause a severe atomic clash with a protein wall. This is why 3D-SynTree uses sub-bin refinement (see [[08_CIRCULAR_STATISTICS_AND_TORSION_HEADS/05_Sub-Bin Pocket-Conditioned Dihedral Refinement]]).

- **Common pitfall:** Using standard MSE loss for dihedral prediction. This fails at the −π/π boundary and doesn't respect circular topology.

- **Common pitfall:** Treating all rotatable bonds as equally flexible. Bonds adjacent to aromatic rings or carbonyls have higher barriers due to conjugation.

### 7. Project Connection

The dihedral angle concepts map directly to the 3D-SynTree codebase:

- **`target_dihedral` tensor** in `syntree/data/fragmenter.py`: Extracted via `ConformerEngine.get_dihedral()`, provides the ground-truth torsion angle for training supervision.

- **`ContinuousTorsionHead`** in `syntree/models/torsion_head.py`: Predicts von Mises parameters (μ, κ) for the dihedral angle of the newly formed bond.

- **`rdMolTransforms.SetDihedralDeg`**: RDKit function that applies the predicted dihedral angle to the 3D conformer.

- **`ConformerEngine.get_dihedral()`** in `syntree/chemistry/conformer.py`: Extracts dihedral angles from 3D conformers for target generation and validation.

### 8. Required Depth

**Essential.** Dihedral prediction is the core 3D capability of 3D-SynTree—the "3D conformational match" that 2D synthesis models lack.
