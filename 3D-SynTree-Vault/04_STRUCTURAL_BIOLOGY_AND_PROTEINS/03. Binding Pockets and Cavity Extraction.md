# 03. Binding Pockets and Cavity Extraction

### 1. What It Is

A **binding pocket** is a concave surface cleft on a protein where small-molecule ligands, substrates, or cofactors bind through non-covalent interactions. Pockets are not random surface features—they are evolutionarily shaped to bind specific molecules with high affinity and specificity.

**Cavity extraction** is the computational process of identifying and isolating the binding pocket from a full protein structure. In 3D-SynTree, pockets are extracted by taking all protein atoms within a Euclidean radius (typically 10.0 Å) of the bound co-crystallized ligand's centroid, then centering the pocket at (0, 0, 0) for translation invariance.

### 2. Why It Exists

Proteins concentrate their catalytic residues and binding functionality in concave pockets rather than on flat, solvent-exposed surfaces. This is because:

- **Concave geometry** provides multiple contact points simultaneously, increasing binding avidity
- **Exclusion of bulk water** reduces entropic penalty of binding (the hydrophobic effect)
- **Pre-organized residues** are already positioned for optimal interaction geometry
- **Shape complementarity** allows van der Waals contacts across the entire pocket surface

For 3D-SynTree, processing an entire protein (often >30,000 atoms) is computationally prohibitive for GPU-based neural networks. Extracting a 10 Å pocket (typically 200–800 atoms) makes the problem tractable while retaining all chemically relevant interactions.

### 3. How It Works

#### Pocket Extraction Protocol

1. **Identify the bound ligand:** Find the co-crystallized ligand in the PDB file (usually the largest non-protein, non-solvent molecule)

2. **Compute ligand centroid:** Average the coordinates of all ligand heavy atoms

3. **Select pocket atoms:** Include all protein atoms within 10.0 Å of the ligand centroid

4. **Filter artifacts:** Remove crystallization additives (GOL, PEG, EDO, DMSO), buffers, and disordered residues

5. **Center at origin:** Translate all coordinates so the pocket centroid is at (0, 0, 0)

6. **Trim to size:** Cap at max_pocket_atoms (700 in 3D-SynTree) to prevent CUDA out-of-memory

7. **Assign charges:** Apply PDB2PQR physiological ionization (Asp/Glu −1, Arg/Lys +1)

#### Pocket Properties

| Property | Description | Typical Range |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| Pocket volume | Cavity space available for ligand | 100–1000 Å³ |
| SASA | Solvent-accessible surface area | Variable |
| Atom count | Number of protein atoms in pocket | 200–800 |
| Subpockets | Distinct sub-regions within pocket | 1–4 |
| Depth | Distance from pocket mouth to bottom | 5–15 Å |

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Binding pocket | Concave protein surface cleft for ligand binding |
| Ligand centroid | Average position of bound ligand atoms |
| Extraction radius | Sphere radius around centroid (10.0 Å in 3D-SynTree) |
| Pocket centering | Translation to origin (0, 0, 0) |
| Subpocket | Distinct sub-region within a larger pocket |
| SASA | Solvent-accessible surface area |
| max_pocket_atoms | Size cap to prevent GPU OOM (700) |
| Translation invariance | Property that model output doesn't depend on pocket position |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **10 Å is a design choice, not a physical law.** It captures residues that directly interact with the ligand plus some surrounding context. Smaller radii might miss key interactions; larger radii waste computation on irrelevant residues.

- **Pocket centering enables translation invariance.** The neural network produces the same output whether the pocket is at (0,0,0) or (100,200,300) in lab coordinates. This is essential because different PDB structures place ligands at different absolute positions.

- **Not all pocket atoms interact with the ligand.** Some form the pocket wall but never touch the ligand. They still matter because they define the pocket's shape and electrostatic environment.

- **Common pitfall:** Extracting pockets from apo (unbound) structures. The pocket may be collapsed or different from the holo (bound) conformation. 3D-SynTree uses holo structures with bound ligands.

- **Common pitfall:** Including flexible loops with missing coordinates. Disordered regions break graph construction and must be handled or excluded.

### 7. Project Connection

The pocket extraction concepts map directly to the 3D-SynTree codebase:

- **`standardize_pocket()`** in `syntree/data/curation.py`: Standardizes pocket coordinates, centers at origin, and prepares for neural network input.

- **`extract_protein_pocket()`** in `syntree/data/multidataset.py`: Extracts 10 Å pockets from PDB structures with ligand centroids.

- **`max_pocket_atoms = 700`** in `syntree/engine/trainer.py`: Size cap to prevent CUDA out-of-memory spikes during training.

- **`BipartitePaiNN`** in `syntree/models/bipartite.py`: Processes the extracted pocket as a 3D point cloud with radius-graph edges.
