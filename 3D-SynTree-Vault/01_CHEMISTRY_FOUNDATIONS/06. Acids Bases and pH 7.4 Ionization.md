# 06. Acids Bases and pH 7.4 Ionization

### 1. What It Is

Acid-base chemistry is the gain or loss of protons (H⁺) by chemical functional groups in aqueous solution. Whether a group is protonated or deprotonated depends on two factors: the pH of the solution and the group's intrinsic acid dissociation constant (pKa).

The **Henderson-Hasselbalch equation** governs this equilibrium:

$$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$$

Where:
- [A⁻] is the concentration of the deprotonated (conjugate base) form
- [HA] is the concentration of the protonated (acid) form
- pKa is the pH at which 50% of the molecules are protonated and 50% are deprotonated

At physiological pH (~7.4), different functional groups exist in predictable ionization states. Carboxylic acids are deprotonated (negatively charged), aliphatic amines are protonated (positively charged), and many heterocycles are neutral.

### 2. Why It Exists

Biological systems operate in water buffered at pH ≈ 7.4. Every drug molecule and every protein binding pocket exists in a specific ionization state at this pH. These charges are not incidental—they are fundamental to how molecules interact:

- **Salt bridges:** Full ionic interactions between oppositely charged groups (e.g., ligand carboxylate with pocket arginine) contribute 3–8 kcal/mol of binding energy
- **Hydrogen bonding:** Ionized groups are stronger hydrogen bond donors and acceptors than neutral ones
- **Solubility:** Charged molecules are generally more water-soluble than neutral ones
- **Membrane permeability:** Highly charged molecules cannot cross lipid bilayers passively

For 3D-SynTree, the stakes are particularly high: PDB crystal structures typically omit hydrogens and assign neutral formal charges to all atoms. If an AI model trains on neutral atoms, it cannot learn salt bridges—yet salt bridges are among the strongest interactions in protein-ligand binding. The model must be explicitly taught physiological ionization.

### 3. How It Works

#### Ionization at pH 7.4

| Functional Group | Typical pKa | State at pH 7.4 | Charge |
|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|--------|
| Carboxylic acid (–COOH) | ~3.5–4.5 | Deprotonated (–COO⁻) | −1 |
| Aliphatic amine (–NH₂, –NHR) | ~9.5–10.5 | Protonated (–NH₃⁺, –NH₂R⁺) | +1 |
| Aromatic amine / aniline | ~4.0–4.5 | Mostly neutral | 0 |
| Phenol (–OH on aryl) | ~10.0 | Mostly neutral | 0 |
| Sulfonic acid (–SO₃H) | < 1 | Fully deprotonated | −1 |
| Guanidinium (–NHC(=NH)NH₂) | ~12.5 | Fully protonated | +1 |
| Imidazole (histidine side chain) | ~6.0–6.5 | Partially protonated | 0 or +1 |

#### The Henderson-Hasselbalch Calculation

For a carboxylic acid with pKa = 4.0 at pH 7.4:

$$7.4 = 4.0 + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$$
$$\log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]} = 3.4$$
$$\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]} = 10^{3.4} \approx 2512$$

So approximately 99.96% of carboxylic acids are deprotonated (charged) at pH 7.4.

For an aliphatic amine with pKa = 10.5 at pH 7.4:

$$7.4 = 10.5 + \log\frac{[\text{B}]}{[\text{BH}^+]}$$
$$\log\frac{[\text{B}]}{[\text{BH}^+]} = -3.1$$
$$\frac{[\text{B}]}{[\text{BH}^+]} = 10^{-3.1} \approx 0.0008$$

So approximately 99.92% of aliphatic amines are protonated (charged) at pH 7.4.

#### Zwitterions

Molecules with both acidic and basic groups can exist as **zwitterions**—dipolar species with both positive and negative charges but net zero charge. Amino acids are the classic example: at pH 7.4, they have a protonated amine (–NH₃⁺) and a deprotonated carboxyl (–COO⁻).

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| pKa | Acid dissociation constant; pH at which 50% is ionized |
| pH | Negative log of hydrogen ion concentration |
| Conjugate base (A⁻) | Deprotonated form of an acid |
| Conjugate acid (HA) | Protonated form of a base |
| Formal charge | Integer charge assigned to an atom (+1, 0, −1) |
| Zwitterion | Molecule with both + and − charges, net zero |
| Physiological pH | pH ≈ 7.4 of biological fluids |
| PDB2PQR | Standardized protocol for assigning physiological charges to PDB structures |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **PDB files are misleading about charge.** Crystal structures usually omit hydrogen atoms and assign neutral formal charges to all heavy atoms. An aspartate appears as neutral COOH rather than COO⁻; a lysine appears as neutral NH₂ rather than NH₃⁺. If your model trains on raw PDB charges, it will miss salt bridges entirely.

- **Ionization state affects binding affinity dramatically.** A salt bridge between a ligand carboxylate and a pocket arginine can contribute 3–8 kcal/mol—enough to change binding by 2–3 orders of magnitude. A neutral carboxylic acid cannot form this interaction.

- **pKa is context-dependent.** The intrinsic pKa of a group (e.g., 4.0 for acetic acid) shifts in a protein environment. A carboxylate buried in a hydrophobic pocket may have a much higher pKa and remain protonated. 3D-SynTree uses the standard PDB2PQR assignment as a practical approximation.

- **Common pitfall:** Assuming all nitrogens are basic. Pyrrole nitrogens and amide nitrogens are not basic—their lone pairs are delocalized and unavailable for protonation. Only aliphatic amines, guanidines, and some heterocycles are protonated at pH 7.4.

- **Common pitfall:** Confusing partial charges (δ+, δ− from bond polarity) with formal charges (+1, −1 from ionization). Both matter, but they arise from different mechanisms and are computed differently.

### 7. Project Connection

The pH 7.4 ionization concepts map directly to the 3D-SynTree codebase:

- **`assign_pocket_formal_charges()`** in `syntree/data/featurizer.py`: Applies PDB2PQR physiological ionization rules—Asp/Glu assigned −1.0, Arg/Lys assigned +1.0, protonated His +1.0. This converts neutral PDB atoms into charged species that can form salt bridges.

- **`pocket_charge` and `ligand_charge` tensors** in `syntree/models/bipartite.py`: These charge tensors enter the BipartitePaiNN encoder via `self.charge_proj(charge)`, allowing the model to learn electrostatic complementarity between ligand and pocket.

- **Salt bridge reward** in `syntree/engine/rl.py`: The `count_key_interactions()` function rewards salt bridges between formally charged ligand atoms and oppositely charged pocket residues within 4.0 Å.
