# 02. Kinetic and Affinity Metrics IC50 Kd Ki

### 1. What It Is

Affinity and kinetic metrics quantify how tightly and how dynamically a small molecule binds to a biological target. These are the standard experimental measurements used in pharmacology to compare drug potency.

| Metric | Symbol | Definition | Units |
|--------|--------|------------|-------|
| Dissociation constant | K_D | [P][L]/[PL] at equilibrium | M (Molar) |
| Inhibition constant | K_i | Thermodynamic affinity for enzyme | M |
| Half-maximal inhibitory concentration | IC₅₀ | Concentration inhibiting 50% activity | M |
| Association rate | k_on | Rate of complex formation | M⁻¹s⁻¹ |
| Dissociation rate | k_off | Rate of complex dissociation | s⁻¹ |

### 2. Why It Exists

These metrics provide standardized, comparable numbers for drug potency across different laboratories, assays, and chemical series. They answer the question: "How much drug do I need to achieve a given level of target engagement?"

For 3D-SynTree, these metrics are the ultimate ground truth. Computational docking scores (kcal/mol) are approximations; experimental K_D, K_i, and IC₅₀ values are what regulators and clinicians care about.

### 3. How It Works

#### Dissociation Constant (K_D)

$$K_D = \frac{k_{\text{off}}}{k_{\text{on}}} = \frac{[P][L]}{[PL]}$$

At equilibrium, K_D equals the free ligand concentration at which half the protein is bound. Smaller K_D means tighter binding:

| K_D Range | Potency Classification |
|-----------|----------------------|
| < 1 nM | Ultra-potent |
| 1–100 nM | Potent (typical drug target) |
| 100 nM – 1 µM | Moderate |
| 1–100 µM | Weak (fragment-level) |
| > 100 µM | Very weak |

#### Inhibition Constant (K_i)

K_i is the absolute thermodynamic affinity of an inhibitor for an enzyme, corrected for substrate concentration. It is the true K_D under defined assay conditions.

#### Half-Maximal Inhibitory Concentration (IC₅₀)

IC₅₀ is the drug concentration needed to inhibit 50% of biological activity in a specific functional assay. Unlike K_D, IC₅₀ is assay-dependent—it depends on substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, and assay format.

The **Cheng-Prusoff equation** relates IC₅₀ to K_i:

$$K_i = \frac{\text{IC}_{50}}{1 + [S]/K_m}$$

for competitive inhibitors, where [S] is substrate concentration and K_m is the Michaelis constant.

#### Binding Kinetics

- **k_on (association rate):** How fast the ligand finds and binds the target. Limited by diffusion (~10⁶–10⁸ M⁻¹s⁻¹) and conformational selection.
- **k_off (dissociation rate):** How fast the ligand leaves the target. Determines residence time (t_R = 1/k_off).
- **Residence time:** The average time a ligand remains bound. Long residence time can correlate with in vivo efficacy even when K_D is moderate.

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| K_D | Equilibrium dissociation constant |
| K_i | Thermodynamic inhibition constant |
| IC₅₀ | Functional assay potency metric |
| k_on | Association rate constant |
| k_off | Dissociation rate constant |
| Residence time | 1/k_off, duration of target engagement |
| Cheng-Prusoff equation | Converts IC₅₀ to K_i |
| Dose-response curve | Activity vs. concentration, sigmoidal |
| pK_D | −log₁₀(K_D), logarithmic affinity scale |
| pIC₅₀ | −log₁₀(IC₅₀), logarithmic potency scale |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **K_D is thermodynamic, IC₅₀ is functional.** K_D measures binding at equilibrium; IC₅₀ measures activity inhibition in an assay. They are related but not identical.

- **Computational docking scores ≠ experimental K_D.** A docking score of −10 kcal/mol might correspond to K_D in the nM range by the scoring function's internal calibration, but actual experimental K_D could be µM or worse. Docking ranks; experiments validate.

- **Concentration regimes matter.** nM potency is typically needed for oral drugs; µM potency may suffice for topical or local administration; mM is generally too weak for therapeutics.

- **Residence time can matter more than K_D.** A drug with moderate K_D but very slow k_off (long residence time) can be more effective in vivo than a drug with tighter K_D but fast k_off.

- **Common pitfall:** Comparing IC₅₀ values from different assays. IC₅₀ depends on substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, and detection method—only compare values from identical assay formats.

- **Common pitfall:** Assuming K_D predicts in vivo efficacy. Cellular permeability, metabolic stability, and protein binding all affect whether a high-affinity binder actually works in a living system.
