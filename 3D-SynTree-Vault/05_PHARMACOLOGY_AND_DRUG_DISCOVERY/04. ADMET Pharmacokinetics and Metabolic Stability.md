# 04. ADMET Pharmacokinetics and Metabolic Stability

### 1. What It Is

**ADMET** describes what the human body does to a drug over time:

- **A**bsorption: Crossing biological membranes into the bloodstream
- **D**istribution: Moving through tissues to reach the target organ
- **M**etabolism: Chemical alteration by liver enzymes
- **E**xcretion: Removal from the body via kidneys or bile
- **T**oxicity: Adverse reactions or cell damage

Over 50% of historical drug candidates failed in clinical trials due to poor ADMET properties, not lack of efficacy.

### 2. Why It Exists

A drug must survive the journey from administration to target engagement. Even a molecule with perfect binding affinity fails if it cannot be absorbed, is cleared too quickly, or is toxic.

For 3D-SynTree, ADMET considerations directly inform the Fsp³ constraint. Flat aromatic molecules are readily oxidized by liver enzymes (CYP450), while 3D-saturated frameworks resist metabolic oxidation.

### 3. How It Works

#### Absorption

Drugs cross membranes primarily by **passive transcellular diffusion** through the lipid bilayer. Key determinants:
- **Lipophilicity:** Moderate log P (1–3) optimal; too low = poor membrane partitioning, too high = poor aqueous solubility
- **Molecular weight:** Smaller molecules diffuse faster
- **Polar surface area:** TPSA < 140 Å² favors permeability
- **Active transport:** P-glycoprotein (P-gp) can pump drugs out of cells

#### Distribution

After absorption, drugs distribute through tissues. **Plasma protein binding** (especially to human serum albumin) sequesters drug, leaving only the **free fraction (f_u)** available to engage targets.

#### Metabolism

The **Cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzyme superfamily** in the liver performs Phase I metabolism (oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis). Major isoforms:
- **CYP3A4:** Metabolizes ~50% of all drugs
- **CYP2D6:** Important for many CNS drugs
- **CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP1A2:** Other major contributors

**Metabolic soft spots** are vulnerable locations:
- Unhindered benzylic carbons
- Exposed aromatic rings (especially electron-rich)
- Labile esters and amides
- N-dealkylation sites

**Phase II metabolism** conjugates metabolites with glucuronic acid, sulfate, or glutathione for excretion.

#### Excretion

Drugs are eliminated via:
- **Renal filtration:** Glomerular filtration of small, water-soluble molecules
- **Biliary excretion:** Larger molecules excreted in bile/feces

#### Toxicity

Key liabilities:
- **hERG channel blockade:** Causes QT prolongation and cardiac arrhythmia
- **Reactive metabolites:** CYP oxidation can produce reactive intermediates that damage proteins/DNA
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver injury from drug or metabolite

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Absorption | Entry into bloodstream |
| Distribution | Movement through tissues |
| Metabolism | Chemical alteration (primarily liver CYP450) |
| Excretion | Removal via kidneys or bile |
| Toxicity | Adverse reactions or cell damage |
| Half-life (t₁/₂) | Time for concentration to decrease by half |
| Clearance (CL) | Volume of blood cleared per unit time |
| Volume of distribution (V_d) | Apparent volume drug distributes into |
| Bioavailability (F) | Fraction of dose reaching systemic circulation |
| CYP3A4 | Major drug-metabolizing enzyme |
| Metabolic soft spot | Vulnerable oxidation site |
| hERG | Potassium channel, cardiotoxicity liability |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **Fsp³ mitigates metabolic clearance.** Flat aromatic rings are easily oxidized by CYP450 enzymes. sp³-rich, saturated frameworks (spirocycles, bicyclics) resist oxidation and improve half-life.

- **High lipophilicity causes non-specific binding.** log P > 4 leads to high plasma protein binding, leaving little free drug available for target engagement.

- **ADMET is not binary.** Properties exist on spectra. A compound with moderate clearance may still be useful with appropriate dosing.

- **Common pitfall:** Optimizing potency at the expense of ADMET. A nM binder that is cleared in 10 minutes is not a drug.

- **Common pitfall:** Assuming in vitro microsome stability predicts in vivo half-life. Liver microsomes lack full physiological context (blood flow, transporters, extra-hepatic metabolism).
