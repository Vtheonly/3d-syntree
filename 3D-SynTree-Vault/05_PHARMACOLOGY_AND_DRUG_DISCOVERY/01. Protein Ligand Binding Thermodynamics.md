# 01. Protein Ligand Binding Thermodynamics

### 1. What It Is

Protein-ligand binding thermodynamics describes the physical laws governing whether a small molecule (ligand) will spontaneously bind to a protein pocket. The central quantity is the **Gibbs free energy of binding**:

$$\Delta G_{\text{bind}} = \Delta H_{\text{bind}} - T\Delta S_{\text{bind}} = -RT \ln K_a = RT \ln K_D$$

Where:
- ΔG_bind: Gibbs free energy change upon binding (negative = favorable)
- ΔH_bind: Enthalpy change (heat released or absorbed)
- T: Absolute temperature (Kelvin)
- ΔS_bind: Entropy change (disorder change)
- R: Gas constant (1.987 × 10⁻³ kcal·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹)
- K_a: Association constant
- K_D: Dissociation constant (1/K_a)

A drug can only engage its target if the bound state has lower free energy than the unbound state.

### 2. Why It Exists

Binding is a competition between the bound state and the unbound state. For a ligand to stay in a pocket, the bound state must be thermodynamically favored. This determines:

- **Affinity:** How tightly the ligand binds (K_D)
- **Selectivity:** Whether it binds the intended target over others
- **Residence time:** How long it stays bound (kinetic, related to thermodynamics)
- **Dosing:** How much drug is needed to occupy enough targets

For 3D-SynTree, binding thermodynamics is the physical foundation of the reward function. Every component of the reward (docking score, contact energy, clash penalty, drug-likeness) is ultimately a proxy for ΔG_bind.

### 3. How It Works

#### Enthalpy (ΔH)

Favorable enthalpy comes from forming stronger interactions in the bound state than existed in the unbound state:

- **Hydrogen bonds:** Directed, distance-dependent interactions (~1–5 kcal/mol each)
- **Salt bridges:** Strong ionic interactions between opposite charges (~3–8 kcal/mol)
- **van der Waals contacts:** Close atomic packing (~0.1–1 kcal/mol per contact)
- **π-interactions:** Aromatic stacking and cation-π interactions

Unfavorable enthalpy comes from:
- **Desolvation penalty:** Stripping water from polar groups costs energy
- **Strain:** Distorting ligand or protein from ideal geometry

#### Entropy (−TΔS)

Entropy is the balance of order and disorder:

**Unfavorable (negative ΔS, positive −TΔS):**
- Ligand loses conformational freedom (rotatable bonds freeze)
- Protein side chains may become ordered
- Translation and rotation are lost upon binding

**Favorable (positive ΔS, negative −TΔS):**
- **Hydrophobic effect:** Ordered water molecules are released from the pocket into bulk solvent, increasing solvent entropy
- **Counterion release:** Charged groups binding may release associated ions

The net entropy change is usually unfavorable for binding (molecules lose freedom), but the hydrophobic effect can provide a favorable contribution.

#### Enthalpy-Entropy Compensation

Making stronger interactions (more favorable ΔH) often requires more ordering (less favorable ΔS). These effects partially cancel, making binding affinity a delicate balance.

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Symbol | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Gibbs free energy | ΔG_bind | Net driving force for binding |
| Enthalpy | ΔH | Heat change from interactions |
| Entropy | ΔS | Disorder change |
| Temperature | T | Absolute temperature (Kelvin) |
| Gas constant | R | 1.987 × 10⁻³ kcal·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ |
| Association constant | K_a | [PL]/([P][L]) at equilibrium |
| Dissociation constant | K_D | 1/K_a, lower = tighter binding |
| Desolvation penalty | — | Energy cost of removing water |
| Conformational freezing | — | Entropy cost of losing flexibility |
| Hydrophobic effect | — | Entropy gain from water release |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **ΔG < 0 means spontaneous binding.** At equilibrium, the ratio of bound to unbound is determined by exp(−ΔG/RT). A ΔG of −10 kcal/mol corresponds to K_D in the nanomolar range.

- **Flexible molecules pay an entropic penalty.** Every rotatable bond that freezes upon binding costs entropy (~0.5–1.5 kcal/mol per bond). 3D-SynTree's small step count (3–4 synthons) and rigid fragment choices minimize this penalty compared to fully flexible diffusion models.

- **Docking scores are approximate ΔG surrogates.** Programs like Vina/GNINA estimate binding energy but omit entropy, solvation, and protein flexibility. They rank molecules reasonably but don't predict absolute K_D accurately.

- **Common pitfall:** Confusing ΔG (thermodynamics, affinity) with k_on/k_off (kinetics, rates). A molecule can bind tightly (low K_D) because it binds fast or because it dissociates slowly—these have different implications for drug action.

- **Common pitfall:** Ignoring desolvation. A ligand with many polar groups pays a high desolvation penalty that can outweigh favorable interactions in the pocket.

The binding thermodynamics concepts map directly to the 3D-SynTree codebase:

- **Multi-objective reward design** in `syntree/engine/rl.py`: The `ThreeDReward` class combines docking score, contact energy, clash penalty, drug-likeness, and key interactions into a scalar reward that proxies ΔG_bind.
