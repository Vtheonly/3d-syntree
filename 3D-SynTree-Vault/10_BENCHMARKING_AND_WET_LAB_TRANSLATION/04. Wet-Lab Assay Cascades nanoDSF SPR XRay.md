# 04. Wet-Lab Assay Cascades nanoDSF SPR XRay

### 1. What It Is

A **wet-lab assay cascade** is a sequence of experimental tests that validate computationally designed molecules. Each assay filters out molecules that fail specific criteria, with later assays being more expensive and lower throughput.

| Assay | What It Measures | Throughput | Cost |
|-------|-----------------|------------|------|
| nanoDSF | Thermal stability / binding-induced stabilization | High | Low |
| SPR | Binding kinetics (k_on, k_off, K_D) | Medium | Medium |
| X-ray crystallography | Atomic-resolution 3D structure of complex | Low | High |
| ITC | Thermodynamics (ΔH, ΔS, K_D) | Low | Medium |
| Cell-based assay | Biological activity in cells | Medium | High |
| ADMET | Metabolic stability, toxicity | Medium | High |

### 2. Why It Exists

Computational design generates candidate molecules, but only experiments can confirm they actually bind and work. The cascade approach:

1. **Screens many molecules cheaply** (nanoDSF)
2. **Confirms binding quantitatively** (SPR)
3. **Validates the binding mode** (X-ray crystallography)
4. **Measures thermodynamics** (ITC)
5. **Tests biological activity** (cell-based assays)
6. **Assesses drug-likeness** (ADMET)

This prevents wasting expensive low-throughput assays on molecules that fail early screens.

### 3. How It Works

#### nanoDSF (Nano Differential Scanning Fluorimetry)

Measures protein melting temperature (T_m). Ligand binding typically stabilizes the protein, increasing T_m. A shift of ≥ 2°C suggests binding.

- **Throughput:** ~384 samples per run
- **Time:** ~1 hour
- **Output:** ΔT_m (temperature shift)

#### SPR (Surface Plasmon Resonance)

Immobilizes protein on a sensor chip, flows ligand over it, measures binding in real-time.

- **Throughput:** ~96 samples per run
- **Time:** Several hours
- **Output:** k_on, k_off, K_D

#### X-Ray Crystallography

Co-crystallizes protein with ligand, determines atomic-resolution structure.

- **Throughput:** ~1-5 structures per project
- **Time:** Weeks to months
- **Output:** 3D structure with atomic coordinates

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| nanoDSF | Thermal shift assay for binding detection |
| SPR | Surface plasmon resonance for binding kinetics |
| X-ray crystallography | Atomic-resolution structure determination |
| ITC | Isothermal titration calorimetry for thermodynamics |
| Assay cascade | Sequential filtering of candidates |
| Hit | Molecule passing initial screens |
| Lead | Molecule progressing through cascade |
| Clinical candidate | Molecule approved for human trials |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **Computational predictions need experimental validation.** A great docking score doesn't guarantee actual binding.

- **The cascade saves money.** Testing 1,000 molecules by nanoDSF is cheap; testing 1,000 by X-ray is impossible.

- **X-ray is the gold standard.** It shows exactly how the ligand binds, validating the computational model's predictions.

- **Common pitfall:** Skipping the cascade and going straight to expensive assays. Most computationally designed molecules fail early.

- **Common pitfall:** Trusting one assay. nanoDSF can give false positives (aggregation artifacts); SPR can give false negatives (mass transport limitations).
