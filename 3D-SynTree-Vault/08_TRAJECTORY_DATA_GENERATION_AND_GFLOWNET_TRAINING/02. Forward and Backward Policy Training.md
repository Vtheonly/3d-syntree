# 02. Forward and Backward Policy Training

### 1. What It Is

In GFlowNet, the **forward policy** P_F generates molecules by making sequential decisions (attach here, add this synthon, set this torsion). The **backward policy** P_B proposes reverse trajectories by decomposing a completed molecule back into its precursor states.

Both policies are trained simultaneously using flow matching losses that enforce detailed balance: the probability of generating a molecule through the forward policy, multiplied by its flow, should equal the probability of reaching it through the backward policy.

### 2. Why It Exists

Standard reinforcement learning optimizes expected reward but doesn't guarantee diverse sampling. GFlowNets instead learn to sample molecules **proportional to their reward**. This means:

- High-reward molecules are sampled frequently
- Low-reward molecules are sampled rarely
- All modes of the reward landscape are covered (no mode collapse)

The backward policy is essential for computing the flow matching loss. Without it, we can't enforce the detailed balance condition that guarantees reward-proportional sampling.

### 3. How It Works

#### Forward Policy

At each state s_t, the forward policy P_F(a | s_t) outputs probabilities for:
- Attachment atom selection
- Synthon selection  
- Reaction family selection
- Torsion angle prediction

The trajectory probability is the product:
$$P_F(\tau) = \prod_t P_F(a_t | s_t)$$

#### Backward Policy

At each state s_t (going backward from the final molecule), the backward policy P_B(s_{t-1} | s_t) outputs probabilities for reversing each action:
- Which bond was formed (reverse of attachment)
- Which synthon was added (reverse of synthon selection)
- What the precursor core was

The backward trajectory probability:
$$P_B(\tau) = \prod_t P_B(s_{t-1} | s_t)$$

#### Flow Matching Loss

The GFlowNet flow F(s) at each state satisfies:
$$F(s) = \sum_{a} F(s') \cdot P_F(a | s)$$

The detailed balance condition:
$$F(s) \cdot P_F(a | s) = F(s') \cdot P_B(s | s')$$

The loss minimizes the violation of this condition across all transitions in sampled trajectories.

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Forward policy P_F | Generates molecules step by step |
| Backward policy P_B | Decomposes molecules step by step |
| Flow F(s) | Total flow through each state |
| Detailed balance | Forward flow = backward flow at each transition |
| Flow matching loss | Minimizes detailed balance violation |
| Trajectory probability | Product of per-step policy probabilities |
| Reward-proportional sampling | Sample molecules proportional to reward |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **GFlowNets sample proportional to reward, not maximize reward.** This is different from standard RL. The goal is to cover all high-reward regions of chemical space, not find the single best molecule.

- **The backward policy is learned, not fixed.** It's a neural network that learns to reverse the forward policy's actions. It must be trained jointly with the forward policy.

- **Flow conservation is the key constraint.** The total flow into a state must equal the total flow out (except at terminal states where flow equals reward).

- **Common pitfall:** Training only the forward policy. Without the backward policy, there's no flow matching loss and no guarantee of reward-proportional sampling.

- **Common pitfall:** Ignoring terminal state flow. At terminal states, F(s_terminal) = R(s_terminal), which anchors the flow values.
