# 03. Curriculum Learning and Difficulty Scheduling

### 1. What It Is

**Curriculum learning** is a training strategy where the model starts with easy examples and gradually progresses to harder ones, mimicking how humans learn. In 3D-SynTree, difficulty is controlled by:

- **Number of synthesis steps:** Easy = 2 steps, Hard = 4 steps
- **Pocket complexity:** Easy = simple pockets, Hard = complex pockets with multiple subpockets
- **Reward threshold:** Easy = lenient acceptance, Hard = stringent acceptance

**Difficulty scheduling** determines when to advance to harder examples based on model performance.

### 2. Why It Exists

Training a GFlowNet from scratch on full 4-step synthesis in complex pockets is difficult because:

- The credit assignment problem is hard: which of 4 actions led to a good/bad reward?
- Early random exploration rarely produces valid molecules in complex pockets
- The reward landscape is sparse: few random molecules score well

Starting with easier tasks (2-step synthesis in simple pockets) lets the model learn basic chemistry before tackling harder problems.

### 3. How It Works

#### Curriculum Stages in 3D-SynTree

| Stage | Steps | Pocket Complexity | Description |
|-------|-------|-------------------|-------------|
| Stage 1 | 2 | Simple | Basic attachment and one synthon addition |
| Stage 2 | 2 | Moderate | Two synthon additions with torsion |
| Stage 3 | 3 | Moderate | Three-step synthesis with multiple handles |
| Stage 4 | 4 | Complex | Full synthesis in complex pockets |

#### Advancement Criteria

Advance to the next stage when:
- Mean episode reward exceeds threshold
- Success rate (valid molecules) > 80%
- Diversity maintained (no mode collapse)

#### Difficulty Parameters

| Parameter | Easy | Hard |
|-----------|------|------|
| Max steps | 2 | 4 |
| Pocket atom cap | 500 | 700 |
| Handle diversity | Low (3 types) | High (10 types) |
| Reward tolerance | Lenient | Strict |

### 4. What It Consists Of

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Curriculum stage | Current difficulty level |
| Step count | Number of synthesis steps per trajectory |
| Pocket complexity | Structural difficulty of binding pocket |
| Advancement criteria | Performance thresholds for stage progression |
| Difficulty scheduling | Rules for when to increase difficulty |
| Credit assignment | Attributing reward to individual actions |

### 5. What You Must Understand

- **Curriculum learning is not just easier data.** It's a structured progression where each stage builds on skills learned in previous stages.

- **The credit assignment problem gets harder with more steps.** With 4 steps, it's harder to tell which action caused a good final reward than with 2 steps.

- **Advancement should be performance-based, not time-based.** Don't advance after N epochs; advance when the model demonstrates mastery of the current stage.

- **Common pitfall:** Advancing too quickly. The model fails on harder tasks because it hasn't mastered the basics.

- **Common pitfall:** Staying too long on easy stages. The model overfits to simple pockets and doesn't generalize.

### 7. Project Connection

The curriculum learning concepts map directly to the 3D-SynTree codebase:

- **`CurriculumScheduler`** in `syntree/engine/curriculum.py`: Manages stage advancement based on performance metrics.

- **`max_steps` scheduling** in `syntree/engine/curriculum.py`: Controls the number of synthesis steps per trajectory.

- **`syntree/engine/trainer.py`**: Integrates curriculum with training loop, adjusting data generation parameters per stage.

- **`scripts/train_gflownet.py`**: Uses curriculum scheduler during training.

### 8. Required Depth

**Supporting.** Important for training stability and convergence, but the underlying GFlowNet algorithm works without curriculum (just slower).
